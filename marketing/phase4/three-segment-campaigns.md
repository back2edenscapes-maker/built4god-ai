## Campaign B — Investigate the repeated client task

**Audience:** Freelance developer who has repeated the same manual client task and is considering packaging a small tool. **Problem:** Repeated work for one client may not generalize to a market. **Hypothesis:** Separating known client work from assumptions will help identify the next conversation. **Placement:** Existing relevant LinkedIn profile, approved by owner. **Ask:** Which part of your repeated client task changes from one client to the next?

### LinkedIn post draft

A task you repeat for clients can be a product idea. The repeat alone doesn't tell you whether the same solution fits another buyer.

Try making two lists before writing the app.

Known from actual work: what the client supplied, the steps you repeated, the errors you fixed, and the result they needed.

Still assumed: how often other clients encounter it, who approves a purchase, whether they can use the tool without you, and which existing workaround they would replace.

For a hypothetical CSV-review task, checking a standard date format might repeat well. Explaining a client's private business rules may still require judgment. Those are different product scopes.

Use the next conversation to investigate one unknown. Keep client names, files and confidential details out of public examples.

## Campaign C — Make the first offer understandable

**Audience:** First-time software-product seller whose prototype works but who cannot clearly state what a buyer gets and how purchase access works. **Problem:** Launch uncertainty turns into endless polishing. **Hypothesis:** A short offer check helps them find one concrete missing explanation. **Placement:** Existing owner-controlled YouTube channel, with a full useful walkthrough; LinkedIn alternative below. **Ask:** Which purchase step would a first-time buyer still have to guess?

### LinkedIn post draft

Before sending someone a payment link, read the offer as if you have never seen your product.

Can you answer these five questions without opening a chat?

1. What exact files or access do I receive?
2. What is excluded?
3. What is the full price and is it recurring?
4. What happens immediately after payment?
5. How do I recover access, ask for help or request a refund?

This is a clarity check, not a security audit or permission to skip technical testing. A prototype can work while its offer is still confusing.

## Common approval and measurement rules

Publish only one segment campaign first. Owner approval must identify the exact account, content and placement. Before posting, verify account access and current platform rules; no automated posting, DMs, paid boosts, tag storms or manufactured engagement. Record publication URL/time and the exact source link in the existing publication register. Reuse an existing Phase 3 article only when it answers a real question; these campaigns do not require another article library.

Source tags are broad channel labels, not segment-level attribution. `segment` only preselects the free-preview exercise; do not claim the analytics database can assign orders to a segment or person. Avoid publishing all campaigns on the same channel in the same measurement window. Log a campaign manually for interpretation, without collecting customer emails or private purchase references.

Use seven days as a review interval, not a statistical test. Five substantive voluntary responses is an operating learning target, not a promised outcome. If there are no responses, report no feedback observed; don't conclude the product is unwanted without knowing whether relevant people saw it. If visits are present but preview use is absent, check clarity before increasing posting. If preview use produces repeated confusion, revise that step before buying or building anything else. A real first sale requires a verified live order; it is never inferred from a checkout click.
