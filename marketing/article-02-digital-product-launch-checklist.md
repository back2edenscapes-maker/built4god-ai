# A first software-product launch checklist for solo builders

A working buy button is one part of a digital-product launch. The buyer also needs to understand the offer, complete payment, receive the right files, and know where to ask for help. A checklist makes those dependencies visible before you send visitors to the page.

These are practical prompts, not a certification, legal review, or guarantee that someone will buy.

## Explain the deliverable

State who the product helps and what task it supports. Name the formats and list what is included. If it is a download, say so. Make limits clear before checkout: hosted software, coaching, and future updates should appear only if actually included.

Show a real sample. A useful excerpt lets buyers assess the approach without payment or exposure of the full paid bundle. Label fictional examples illustrative. Avoid screenshots implying customer results you have not observed.

Check that the page and checkout use the same name, currency, price, and billing frequency. A $9 one-time download should not appear to be a subscription.

## Open files in a clean environment

Extract the archive and open every file. Verify filenames, encoding, readable text, and start instructions. Explain any external tool requirements.

For CSV worksheets, try a common spreadsheet program or text editor. Do not imply formulas or automation where sheets contain only input columns. Remove private notes, internal links, and unrelated files.

A phone buyer should understand how to save the archive and open it later on a suitable device. A mobile-friendly payment page does not mean editing a large worksheet is comfortable on a small screen.

## Test payment confirmation

Use the provider sandbox. Test successful payment and cancellation. Visiting a success URL must not grant access on its own; the server needs verified payment confirmation.

Test duplicate notifications, delayed success, and invalid notifications. Retries must not duplicate orders or delivery messages. Unpaid or forged events must not unlock files.

Do not submit real charges or refunds solely for testing without explicit authorization. Label sandbox results as tests and exclude them from revenue.

## Check delivery and recovery

After a confirmed sandbox purchase, download the exact archive and check its integrity. Confirm an unpaid visitor cannot obtain the file. Keep delivery references out of public source, analytics, and marketing links.

A buyer can close a browser or lose an email. Provide recovery that verifies ownership without revealing whether an arbitrary address purchased. Support should explain what information helps and warn against sending card details or passwords.

If email delivery is offered, verify the sender, actual delivery status, expiry behavior, and retries. Implementing email code does not prove deliverability. Do not promise automatic email until the configured service is verified.

## Explain support and refunds

Use a monitored support address. Put the real purchase terms and refund process where buyers can find them before payment. State how requests work; do not improvise a promise on launch day.

Test refund effects on future access. Revocation cannot retract an already downloaded file, so do not imply it can. Track refunds separately from gross sales.

## Measure without collecting secrets

Keep visits, preview activity, checkout clicks, verified orders, and refunds separate. Visits are not necessarily unique people. Clicks are not completed payments. Missing analytics means unknown activity, not zero.

Use public source tags for approved placements. Never put buyer email addresses, payment references, or private tokens in them. Leave processor fees unknown until reliable fee data exists; gross revenue is not profit.
